A persistent dilemma plagues university hardware spin-outs: the Principal Investigator (PI) is the singular visionary who truly understands the deep tech, yet asking them to abandon their tenured academic career to become a full-time startup CEO is often unrealistic and counterproductive. Technology Transfer Offices (TTOs) must embrace a new model to bridge this gap: The Fractional Founder.

The traditional Silicon Valley expectation of a founder working 100-hour weeks in a garage does not map well to academic deep tech. By structuring spin-outs to accommodate the "Fractional Founder," TTOs can retain the PI's crucial technical guidance while building a robust, commercially focused leadership team, accelerating the path to market.

### The Trap of the Academic CEO

When a PI attempts to serve as the full-time CEO of a hardware spin-out, the results are frequently suboptimal. PIs are world-class researchers, optimized for scientific discovery, grant writing, and academic publishing. The skills required to scale a hardware startup—supply chain logistics, venture capital fundraising, ruthless product prioritization, and enterprise sales—are entirely different.

When PIs step into the CEO role, the startup often suffers from feature creep, prioritizing scientific novelty over manufacturability and market demand. Furthermore, the university loses a top-tier researcher.

### Engineering the Fractional Founder Model

The Fractional Founder model formally integrates the PI into the startup in a part-time, highly leveraged capacity (typically 1-2 days a week), usually holding the title of Chief Scientific Officer (CSO) or Technical Advisor. To make this work, TTOs must actively facilitate specific structural alignments.

#### 1. Recruiting the Commercial Co-Founder (CEO) Early
The success of the Fractional Founder hinges entirely on pairing them with a seasoned, commercially driven CEO. The TTO should leverage its network, engaging Entrepreneurs in Residence (EIRs) or industry veterans early in the commercialization process (around TRL 4). This commercial CEO takes ownership of fundraising, operations, and market strategy, allowing the PI to focus purely on the core technical hurdles.

#### 2. Defining Clear Boundaries and IP Flow
When a PI works in both the academic lab and the startup, conflict of interest (COI) and IP contamination issues are highly likely. TTOs must establish rigid, transparent boundaries. Clear agreements must dictate that research conducted in the university lab (funded by grants) belongs to the university, while development work conducted on startup time and resources belongs to the company. Master Sponsored Research Agreements (SRAs) can be used to legally channel university lab capabilities to support the startup without muddying IP ownership.

#### 3. Structuring Equity for Part-Time Commitment
Venture capitalists are wary of "part-time founders" holding massive equity stakes. TTOs can help mediate the equity split to reflect the reality of the arrangement. While the PI is the inventor, the commercial team executing the high-risk scale-up also requires significant equity to stay motivated. A common structure involves the PI taking a smaller initial equity stake, coupled with milestone-based grants or options that vest based on their ongoing technical contributions to the company.

#### 4. Managing University COI Policies
University COI policies are often antiquated and designed to prevent any commingling of academic and commercial interests. Progressive TTOs must work with university administration to modernize these policies, explicitly allowing and managing the Fractional Founder arrangement. This requires transparent reporting structures and strict adherence to conflict management plans, ensuring that the PI's academic duties (teaching, advising students) are not compromised by their startup involvement.

### Conclusion

The Fractional Founder model is not a compromise; it is an optimization for deep tech commercialization. By formally structuring the PI's involvement as a high-level technical visionary while empowering a dedicated commercial leadership team, TTOs can maximize the success rate of hardware spin-outs. This approach respects the PI's academic career while providing the startup with the specific operational expertise required to navigate the perilous journey from lab prototype to commercial product.
