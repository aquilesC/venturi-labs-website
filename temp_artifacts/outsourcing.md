# Outsourcing Your Grant: The Risks, Rewards, and Why You Must Own the Process

Applying for major European non-dilutive funding—such as the [EIC Accelerator](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en), [Eurostars](https://www.eurostars-eureka.eu/), or national schemes—is a grueling endeavor. For a deep tech founder or an academic transitioning from the bench, the sheer volume of administrative requirements, mandatory financial tables, and specific European Commission jargon can feel like an entirely new language. 

Because of this immense friction, a massive industry of grant-writing consultancies has emerged. Their pitch is highly appealing: *Hand us your technical paper, and we will write the grant, structure the consortium, and win you millions.*

While these agencies provide a valuable service, completely outsourcing your grant application is a dangerous trap for deep tech hardware startups. Here is a balanced look at the risks, the rewards, and why you must fundamentally own your commercialization process.

## The Rewards: Why You Should Consider a Consultant

For academic founders navigating their first major commercialization effort, good consultancies offer undeniable value in overcoming the "Grant Tax"—the bureaucratic friction of applying for public money.

*   **Translating Science into "EU Parlance":** Evaluators are looking for specific keywords and structural narratives. They want "Impact Pathways," "Knowledge Utilization," and "Key Exploitation Results." A good consultant knows how to translate your TRL 4 prototype into the exact format evaluators are trained to score highly.
*   **Administrative Project Management:** Navigating the [EU Funding & Tenders Portal](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/home), collecting PIC numbers from consortium partners, and ensuring formatting compliance takes weeks of unbillable time. Agencies handle this logistical nightmare efficiently.
*   **Financial Sanity Checks:** Good consultancies have internal finance teams that check your proposed budget against strict intervention rates and eligible cost guidelines, ensuring your application isn't disqualified on a technicality before it even reaches a reviewer.

## The Risks: The Danger of the "Black Box" Approach

The fatal mistake researchers make is assuming a consultancy can invent a business model for them. When you treat grant writing as a completely outsourced "black box," you expose your deep tech venture to severe structural risks.

### 1. The "Generic TAM" Trap
Major consultancies rely on standard market research databases to write the commercialization sections of your grant. If you are building a highly specialized piece of low-volume scientific hardware, they will often struggle to define your true market. To make the grant look attractive, they might inflate your Total Addressable Market (TAM), claiming you are targeting the entire €50B semiconductor industry rather than the specific 50 R&D labs that actually need your tool. If your market narrative is generic, expert reviewers will spot the disconnect immediately.

### 2. Bypassing Customer Discovery
Writing a grant should force you to confront the hardest questions about your technology. *Who exactly is going to buy this? What is their current alternative? Why is our solution worth the switching cost?* 
If an external consultant answers these questions for you, you lose the crucial process of [Customer Discovery](https://steveblank.com/category/customer-development/). You might win the grant, but you will spend public money building a product that nobody wants because you never personally spoke to the end-user.

### 3. Failing the EIC Jury Defense
For high-stakes grants like the EIC Accelerator, winning the written phase is only half the battle. You must then defend your proposal in front of a live jury of seasoned venture capitalists and industry veterans. The jury will not interrogate your consultant; they will interrogate *you*. If you did not intimately craft the pricing strategy, the supply chain mapping, and the competitive analysis, your defense will collapse under pressure.

## The Principle: Own the Core, Outsource the Polish

In deep tech hardware, you cannot outsource your strategy. The Principal Investigator or lead engineer must be deeply intentional about which grants they apply to and what commercialization vehicle they use (e.g., an asset carve-out vs. a VC-backed spin-off). 

If you decide to hire a grant-writing agency, you must treat them as an *accelerator of your ideas*, not the generator of them. 

**How to structure the relationship:**
1.  **You write the User Requirements:** Do not let a consultant guess what the lab needs. You must define the technical milestones and how your prototype crosses the TRL 4 to TRL 8 gap.
2.  **You dictate the Economics:** If your hardware requires low-volume manufacturing to reach profitability, ensure the consultant builds the financial tables around that reality, rather than forcing a hyper-growth VC model that doesn't fit your physics.
3.  **They format, edit, and align:** Let the agency map your ground-truth business model into the specific bureaucratic templates and character limits required by the funding body.

Grants are a mechanism to finance your transition from the bench to the market—they are not the business itself. By actively owning the grant-writing process, you ensure that the roadmap you submit to the funding agency is the exact same roadmap you intend to execute on Monday morning.
